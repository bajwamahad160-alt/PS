# Characters — *The Kettle at the End of the World*

## Wren Auldstove — protagonist

Late forties. Keeper of the Kettle-and-Coal in Lowfen. A former hearthwarden
and, in the reaping years, something worse: one of the people who learned to
pull warmth from the living to hold a line, and did it well enough that
others copied her. She stopped on her own — not caught, not punished, just
*stopped* — and that unpunished quality is the wound. No one made her pay,
so she pays herself, one pot of tea at a time.

- **Wants:** to be left alone with a hearth she keeps honestly.
- **Needs:** to accept that walking away wasn't the same as making it right —
  and that the ember is the bill come due.
- **Voice:** dry, economical, kind in actions and prickly in words. Notices
  temperature the way other people notice sound. Makes very good tea and
  will not tell you how.
- **Power:** a strong, deliberately un-practiced emberwright. She keeps her
  hands cool on purpose. When she finally uses the old skill again, it
  should cost the reader something too.

## Pell — the child

About ten. Arrives in Chapter 1 half-frozen, wordless at first, carrying the
ember in a wrapped bundle she will not let go of. Where she comes from and
why the ember chose *her hands* is the spine of the mystery. Not precious,
not a plot-token: stubborn, watchful, funny in a deadpan way once she thaws,
and carrying a specific grief she doesn't hand over easily.

- **Function:** she is the crack the epic pours through, but she must always
  read as a real, load-bearing kid, never a MacGuffin with legs.

## Osric Vane — Ashguard captain

Fifties. Rode with Wren in the reaping years; the one who stayed in when she
walked out. Believes the reaping years weren't a sin but a *duty deferred*,
and that the ember means the world is offering them a way to do it right.
Genuinely fond of Wren. Genuinely willing to do the unforgivable if the math
says so. The book's most dangerous man because he is the most reasonable.

- **The knife:** he is not wrong that the Grey is coming. He is wrong about
  what a person is allowed to do about it.

## Sister Halla — Chantry hospice-priest

Sixties. Came to Lowfen years ago to sit with its dying and stayed. The
gentle face of the Long Cold: she eases real suffering, and the town loves
her, and she means every kindness. She also believes, all the way down, that
the ember must be *put out* — that a fire that never dies is the cruelest
thing imaginable in a world meant to rest. Her tragedy is that she is the
best person in the book and her conclusion is monstrous.

## The Lowfen regulars (the cozy ensemble)

- **Berd** — the town's smith and unofficial mayor; big, slow, decent;
  standing order of the strongest black tea and two sugars he pretends he
  doesn't take.
- **The Aldous sisters** — Nettie and Fen, weavers, ancient, sharp;
  gossip as public utility; the first to notice anything.
- **Coll** — a young man of nineteen who hasn't left Lowfen yet and is
  ashamed of both wanting to and not wanting to; Wren's part-time help.
- **The empty chair** — kept at the common table's north end. Whose it was
  is a Part One reveal and part of Wren's grief.

## The Grey

Not a character, but written like one: patient, silent, undramatic, and
always exactly one town closer than anyone wants to say out loud.
